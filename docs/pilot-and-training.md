# Pilot and training plan (weeks 7–8)

## Choose the pilot
2–3 barangays: one with good connectivity (e.g. Poblacion), one with patchy connectivity, one with high reported shortage. Name a coordinator and a backup in each. Keep existing phone and walk-in channels running throughout.

## Before day 1
- [ ] Accounts created and tested for every pilot coordinator and the municipal team.
- [ ] Contacts and hall hours entered in Settings; privacy notice approved.
- [ ] Baseline numbers recorded (requests per month through current channels, typical time to respond).
- [ ] QR code printed (Install screen) for barangay halls and posted in Facebook groups / Messenger.

## Training (60–90 minutes, hands-on)
1. The workflow and statuses; what residents see at each status. Notes are written **for the resident**.
2. Verify, refer, close a request; practise on test requests.
3. Contact details: view only when needed; every view is logged.
4. Announcements and schedules: planned vs confirmed vs completed; change notes; completion needs delivery outcome.
5. Daily routine and escalation (see runbook); what to do if the app is down.
6. Entering a request for a resident who walked in or phoned.

## Feedback
Weekly 15-minute check-in. Ask residents (including those with weak internet): could you find help, submit, understand the status, trust the schedule? Ask staff: time per request, confusing screens, missing information.

## Measure (monthly; plan §13)
Requests by barangay · verified and unresolved · average time to verification · time from verification to assistance · litres delivered · fulfilled vs received · schedule accuracy · reach and feedback.
Reminder: this is **reported** demand. People without internet or awareness are not in the numbers.

## Go / no-go for municipality-wide launch
All of: acceptance checklist complete, every pilot request had an owner and an outcome, no unresolved privacy issues, staff confident in the routine, a named owner for daily operation.
